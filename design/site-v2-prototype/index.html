How it works

A swarm is a model shared out across Macs.

Someone starts a swarm

They pick a registered model and a name, and decide who may use it: anyone, people they approve, or invited keys. Their browser key signs the listing. There are no accounts.

Macs join and share the work

Each helper Mac holds a slice of the model's layers. The owner approves every Mac that joins, and a Mac can pause or leave whenever it needs its resources back.

You send a prompt

Your browser sends it straight to the swarm's own entry Mac. It passes through the helper Macs, and the answer streams back token by token.

Privacy, plainly

Who can see what.

This site (the directory)A public list of swarms: names, models, owner key, status. It never sees your prompts, sets no cookies and runs no analytics.
The swarm's MacsThe entry Mac and the helper Macs can read your prompt and the activations. It is not end-to-end encrypted from them. Use swarms you trust.
CloudflareSwarm entries sit behind Cloudflare. It ends the HTTPS connection, so it can see your IP address, timing, and prompts and answers in plaintext.
YouA key made in your browser identifies you to swarms. No email, no password. It never leaves the browser, and you can forget it any time.

Questions

Is this ready to rely on?

No. It is a preview. It has been proven on two Apple silicon Macs the project owns. The public demo swarm runs on one volunteer Mac behind a tunnel and may sleep.

What does "serving" mean?

The swarm's Macs have loaded the model and the route between them has passed its checks. "Warming up" means that is still happening. "Offline" means the swarm stopped reporting.

Why choose a model from a list instead of typing one?

Each model needs a pinned set of files and a checked layer layout, so swarms can only run models Mycelium has registered. The list grows with releases.
