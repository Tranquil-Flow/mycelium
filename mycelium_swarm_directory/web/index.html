What happens to your prompt

  • Who runs a swarm. The owner signs the listing with their key. The people lending their devices run the swarm, not Mycelium.
  • Where it travels. Your browser sends prompts straight to the swarm's entry service, never through this directory. The service can read the prompt in plain text and passes the work to the participating devices. Those devices can read prompts, replies and intermediate model data (activations). Messages are not end-to-end encrypted from them. Do not send secrets.
  • What is stored. The directory and chat page do not store prompts or replies. The directory sets no cookies; your key and access grants stay in this browser. The owner's device keeps the last 64 answers in memory so your browser can fetch them. People running the entry service and participating devices could keep their own logs.
  • Public swarm statistics. Published performance numbers include first-token and request durations, decode tokens per second, stage compute and hop round-trip times (activation transport or owner control probes) when measured, layer ranges, mode, serving time, request totals, sample counts and measurement windows. These are self-reported by the owner app. No prompts, replies, IP addresses, hostnames or node keys are included in the statistics.
  • Your quota. Requests and tokens are limited per key. Limits per network address and a daily cap also apply.

The app asks before downloading models or sharing your device. You can stop sharing at any time.

Stronger privacy protections are a goal as Mycelium develops.

Creating your key…

Forget this browser key?

This deletes the key from this browser, with every grant and open-access allowance saved with it. You can no longer manage the swarms you own with it (their owner key is gone for good), and the access owners gave this key stops working here.

The next visit makes a new key: a new identity with a fresh per-key quota. That does not reset the limits counted by network address, which the swarm's entry applies to everyone, nor a swarm's daily cap for open access.
