AI, grown together

AI on the devices you already own

Our goal is to put capable AI in your hands, on devices you own and share. Together, you could host a model too big for any one device, with the work split across your group.

We're working towards more device types, larger models shared across more devices, and stronger privacy. The current release is the first step in that direction.

What we're building

Four goals for AI inference: running a model to turn your prompt into an answer.

Verifiable

Goal: Check that your answer came from the model you asked for, run honestly, not a cheaper substitute or tampered output.

Why it matters: When strangers' devices run your model, trust needs proof, not promises—including protection against malicious instructions slipped into an answer along the way.

Today: Result verification is in development, not in this release.

Distributed

Goal: Pool capacity without one company or data centre owning it. Run models too big for one device across many.

Why it matters: Shared capacity means more resilience and no single gatekeeper.

Today: The release distributes a model across a swarm of Apple silicon Macs.

Private

Goal: Protect your prompts and data even from the devices doing the work.

Why it matters: People bring their most sensitive questions to AI.

Today: The swarm's devices can read prompts and replies. Messages are not end-to-end encrypted from them. Know who can see your conversation.

Edge devices

Goal: Run and contribute from the phones, laptops and desktops you already have.

Why it matters: Everyone should be able to take part. Lower requirements let you contribute to and use AI without a powerful computer—something much of the world doesn't have. Put idle capacity to work while keeping local control.

Today: Apple silicon Macs are supported; other device types are in development. Current requirements.

Share the work of running a model

How a swarm works

1. Invite people you trust

An owner starts a swarm and sends an invite. Each person sets their device's memory, storage and network limits, chooses when to share, and can stop at any time.

2. Split the work

The app checks what fits, then places parts of the model on the participating devices. They pass intermediate model data between them to produce an answer. A swarm needs at least two devices.

3. Send a prompt to the swarm

The directory lists swarms so you can find them. Your browser sends prompts straight to the swarm's entry service, never through the directory.

Know who can see your conversation.

Choose people you trust

The swarm's devices can read prompts, replies and intermediate model data (activations). Messages are not end-to-end encrypted from those devices. Do not send secrets.

Your prompts stay out of the directory

The directory carries no prompts. You don't need an account, and this site does not track you. Browsing this page creates no browser key; the swarms app stores a key in your browser.

Cloudflare is part of the connection

Cloudflare terminates HTTPS (TLS) for this site and entries it hosts. It can see IP addresses, timing and plaintext traffic at those endpoints.

You decide when to share

Installing the app doesn't start sharing. The app asks before downloading models or sharing your device. Your limits and stop control stay on your device.

Stronger privacy protections are a goal as Mycelium develops.
